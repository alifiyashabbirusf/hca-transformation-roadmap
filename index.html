Three questions to answer before the next AI pilot is funded

This site sequences six of HCA Healthcare's published technology initiatives into a Crawl, Walk, Run roadmap, assigns the decisions that roadmap creates, and shows how a leadership team would review progress each quarter. Read the three pages in this order.

  1. What has to be ready first? The roadmap: three foundations, three pilots, and the gate each pilot must pass before it expands.
  2. Who signs each decision? The RACI: seven recurring decisions the roadmap creates, each with one accountable role.
  3. Is it working? The dashboard: one page a leadership team can read in ten minutes each quarter.

Who this is for

This site is written for the chief operating officer of a regional health system with 10 to 20 hospitals. Clinical and technology leaders have each brought the COO a promising AI tool for nurses or physicians, and the board has asked for one plan: what to prepare first, where to pilot, and when to expand.

This reader does not need a technology primer. They need to know what must be true before money is released for the next step.

What that reader can do with it

  • See which pilot is blocked if a foundation slips If the data platform is late, nurse scheduling can still be piloted, but clinical documentation and nurse handoff cannot.
    Roadmap page
  • Apply a pass or fail test before expanding Each pilot has a measurable gate, such as 20% less schedule-preparation time with at least 80% of nurse managers using the tool.
    Roadmap page
  • Know who signs each recurring decision Funding the next wave, releasing a clinical AI tool from pilot to rollout, and fixing a site that is below its adoption target each have one named owner.
    RACI page
  • Decide in ten minutes whether to fund the next wave An example Q6 scenario shows each metric with its target, a current value and an on track, at risk or off track status, and the page lists the decisions due this quarter.
    Dashboard page

What we added beyond the brief

A health system leader has seen plenty of AI roadmaps. We tried to add the parts that usually go missing.

On the roadmap, Eduardo separated pilot-site readiness from the full enterprise EHR migration, which stays outside the nine-month foundation. It sits in the dependencies section.

On the RACI, Alifiya applied a rule from her years leading textile design, where a designer never approved their own print: the team that does the work does not sign it off. That is why the Chief Clinical Officer, not the pilot team, releases a clinical AI tool. The Chief Executive Officer signs none of the seven decisions on purpose, because a decision that goes to the top every quarter has not been delegated.

On the dashboard, Gerald added a "Safety overrides time savings" box in the Run section, so faster workflows alone never justify expansion if clinical quality, staffing compliance or safety checks fail.

Across all pages, the teal and blue come from surgical scrubs, and red, amber and green appear only on the dashboard, where they report status.
