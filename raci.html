Who signs each decision the roadmap creates

The roadmap prepares three foundations, pilots three tools for care teams, and expands each tool only after it passes a gate. That sequence raises seven decisions that come back every quarter. Each one has a single accountable role.

Alifiya Shabbir, Governance Designer. Group 2, Week 6.

  • AAccountable: signs the decision, one per row
  • RResponsible: prepares it
  • CConsulted: asked before
  • IInformed: told after

Why each role signs

DecisionAccountableWhy this roleWhere the roadmap creates it
D1 Budget allocationEach quarter, how is the budget split between foundation work and the pilot and rollout waves? EVP and Chief Financial Officer The CFO owns capital allocation and is not one of the teams asking for the money, so this role can weigh foundation work against rollout waves fairly. When a pilot misses its target, the CFO is the one who can hold the next wave's funding back. Crawl, Walk and Run. I1, I2 and I3 compete for money with the pilots and waves of I4, I5 and I6.
D2 Architecture standardsMay a hospital or division deviate from the enterprise standard for data feeds or record interfaces? SVP and Chief Information Officer The CIO leads the Information Technology Group, which supports every HCA site and carries the cost when sites diverge from the standard. The division asking for an exception should not approve it, and neither should the team that wants the rollout to keep moving. Crawl and Run. I1 and I2 set the standard. I2 repeats its checks at every new site, and I4-R needs a path for local exceptions.
D3 Data governanceWho may use clinical and workforce data for the three tools, and is a site's data good enough to release a feed? EVP and Chief Clinical Officer The data describes patients and nurses' work, and both belong to the clinical organization this role leads: clinical quality, nursing and clinical informatics. A clinical owner also keeps the decision about who may use the data separate from the CIO, who stores it. Crawl and Walk. I1 sets access controls. I2 validates patient matching for I5 and I6. I4-W needs validated workforce feeds.
D4 Initiative prioritizationWhen rollout capacity is short, which tool and which sites go next, and which division waits? EVP and Chief Operating Officer Putting one division's sites first makes another division wait, so the call has to sit above the divisions. The COO runs operations across all of them, and from April 2024 the three group presidents report to this role. Walk and Run. Pilot sites for I4-W, I5-W and I6-W, then the site waves of I4-R, I5-R and I6-R.
D5 Vendor and partnershipShould a key external relationship be extended, changed or ended, and what may the partner do with HCA data? SVP and Chief Information Officer All three partners supply technology that the Information Technology Group has to run, secure and connect, so the CIO lives with each contract after it is signed. Legal advises on data terms and the CFO on cost, but neither operates the platforms. Crawl, Walk and Run. Google Cloud for I1, MEDITECH for I2 and Augmedix for I5.
D6 Clinical AI release gate HCA-specificDoes a pilot pass its gate into Run, may a new model version be used, and does an incident pause a rollout? EVP and Chief Clinical Officer The roadmap's rule is that a time saving never overrides a clinical safety finding, so the role responsible for clinical quality signs. The team that built the tools prepares the evidence but does not approve its own work. HCA's Responsible AI policy already requires a written go or no-go for high-risk tools. Walk to Run. I3 and every pilot-to-rollout gate: I4-W to I4-R, I5-W to I5-R and I6-W to I6-R.
D7 Adoption remediation HCA-specificWhen a site misses its adoption or benefit target: more training, more time, or withdraw the tool? Division President Low adoption has local causes: staffing, time for training, a unit manager who is not convinced. The Division President controls the staff time and budget where the tool is underused, which head office can advise on but cannot free up. Walk and Run. I4-W needs 80% of eligible nurse managers using the tool. A missed target triggers a review before more funding.

Due at the Q6 review, the end of Walk

  1. Release I4 Timpani nurse scheduling from its pilot (I4-W) into its first rollout wave (I4-R), or hold it? Row D6, signed by the EVP and Chief Clinical Officer.
  2. Fund the first rollout wave now, or keep the money on readiness work? Row D1, signed by the EVP and Chief Financial Officer.
  3. A pilot site is below its adoption target: more training, more time, or withdraw the tool? Row D7, signed by the Division President.

These are proposed decision rights, built from HCA's public titles and the roadmap's gates. HCA does not publish how it assigns these decisions, so each Accountable choice is our own reasoning.

Titles are as printed in the HCA Investor Day speaker bios (November 2023), HCA's fiscal 2022 annual report, its October 2022 reorganization, its Responsible AI policy (October 2024) and HCA hospital leadership pages. The April 2024 reporting change is from Healthcare Dive.
